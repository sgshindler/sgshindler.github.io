For a long time, I've been interested in heuristic methods for modeling physical systems. Heuristic models are models which aren't built off of a fundamental physical principle, like conservation of energy, and they're not built off an underlying mechanism of action, like reaction kientic models. Instead, they are built off a self-evident fact about the mathematics being used. As with many things the greatest strength of a heuristic model is also its greatest weakness. They dont require understanding the system you're modeling to a very high degree, but unfortunately, fitting the model often doesn't give much insight into the underlying physical system. One useful heuristic model that applies to a large number of systems is that of exponential growth or decay. In this post, I'm going to outline why exponential growth and decay is a heuristic, why is appears in so many physical systems, how those underlying principles can be generalized.
